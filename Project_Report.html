1. Introduction & Objective

A coupling is a mechanical device used to connect two rotating shafts for the purpose of transmitting power and torque. Among the various types of couplings, the Rigid Flanged Coupling (Protected Type) is one of the most widely used in industrial machinery due to its simplicity, reliability, and capacity to transmit large torques without relative motion between the connected shafts.

Objective

The objectives of this project are:

  1. Design a complete Rigid Flanged Coupling using the Maximum Shear Stress Theory (Tresca Criterion) in compliance with IS 2693, DIN 116, and IS 2048 standards.
  2. Develop an interactive CAD workstation (web-based) that takes input torque, material properties, and design constraints, and computes all output coupling dimensions with live stress verification and SVG technical drawings.
  3. Implement the same calculation engine in Python and C++ for cross-platform portability and evaluation.
  4. Verify all design outputs with safety factor checks, rigidity checks, and constraint compliance.

2. Theory & Design Fundamentals

2.1 What is a Rigid Flanged Coupling?

A rigid flanged coupling consists of two flanges, each keyed to the respective shaft, and connected together by a set of bolts arranged on a pitch circle. The "protected" type features a raised rim on the outer periphery of each flange that covers the bolt heads and nuts, thereby protecting them from damage and preventing accidental contact.

The main components of a rigid flanged coupling are:

  • Shaft: The rotating member that transmits the torque. Its diameter is the fundamental design parameter from which all other dimensions are derived.
  • Hub: The enlarged cylindrical portion of the flange that fits over the shaft. It transmits torque from the shaft to the flange via the key.
  • Flange: The disc portion that mates with the other half of the coupling. Typically made of cast iron (FG 200 or FG 260).
  • Sunk Key: A rectangular key fitted into keyways cut in both the shaft and hub. It transmits torque via shear and crushing.
  • Bolts: A set of bolts arranged uniformly on a pitch circle diameter (PCD) to clamp the two flanges together and transmit torque via shear.
  • Protective Rim: A raised ring on the outer circumference of the flange that encloses the bolt heads.

2.2 Failure Criterion — Maximum Shear Stress Theory (Tresca)

For ductile materials such as carbon steels and alloy steels used for the shaft, key, and bolts, the Maximum Shear Stress Theory (MSST / Tresca Criterion) is the appropriate failure criterion. This theory states that failure (yielding) occurs when the maximum shear stress in the member equals the shear stress at yield in a simple tension test:

Tresca Criterion — Permissible Stresses
Allowable Shear Stress:
    τ_allow = Syt / (2 × FoS)

Allowable Crushing (Compressive) Stress:
    σc,allow = Syt / FoS

Where:
    Syt = Tensile yield strength of the material (MPa)
    FoS = Factor of Safety (typically 2.5 – 4.0 for static design)

For brittle materials like Grey Cast Iron used for flanges, the permissible shear stress is a stated design limit: τci = 15.0 MPa.

2.3 Design Constraints & Boundary Conditions

Two design constraints govern the overall feasibility:

Constraint Symbol Nature Description
Max Shaft Diameter Dmax Spatial Envelope (Input) Maximum allowable shaft diameter dictated by housing/clearance. If calculated D > Dmax, a stronger material must be selected.
Max Angle of Twist θmax Rigidity Standard (Input) Maximum torsional twist per meter span (standard: 1.0° per 1000 mm). If θ > θmax, shaft is upsized for stiffness.
Key Design Principle: The constraints Dmax and θmax are user-specified boundary limits. The actual shaft diameter D and angle of twist θ are mechanically calculated from the applied Torque T and material properties (Syt, G). If θ exceeds θmax, D is automatically recalibrated upward. If D exceeds Dmax, the user must select a stronger material.

3. Design Formulas & Calculation Procedure

The design proceeds step-by-step from the shaft diameter as the primary output, and all subsequent component dimensions are proportional functions of D. This section documents every formula used in the calculation engine.

3.1 Shaft Design (Torsional Shear Strength)

The shaft diameter is determined from the torsion equation for a solid circular shaft:

Shaft Diameter from Torsional Strength
T = (π / 16) × τallow × d³

Solving for d:
d = [ (16 × T) / (π × τallow) ]1/3

Where:
    T = Transmitted torque (N·mm)
    τallow = Syt / (2 × FoS) — Permissible shear stress (MPa)

The calculated d is rounded up to the nearest standard diameter (multiple of 5 mm, minimum 20 mm):
D = max(20, ceil(d / 5) × 5)

The induced torsional shear stress in the adopted shaft is then verified:

Induced Shear Stress Verification
τshaft = (16 × T) / (π × D³)   ≤   τallow

3.2 Torsional Rigidity Check

Even if the shaft is strong enough, it must be stiff enough. The angle of twist over a standard 1-meter span is checked:

Torsional Rigidity — Angle of Twist
Polar Moment of Inertia:   J = (π / 32) × D⁴

Angle of Twist:   θ = (T × L) / (J × G) × (180 / π)   degrees

Where:
    L = 1000 mm (standard verification span)
    G = Shear modulus of the shaft material (MPa)

Criterion: θ ≤ θmax (standard: 1.0° per meter)

If θ > θmax — Rigidity Recalibration:
Dtwist = [ (32 × T × L) / (π × G × θmax,rad) ]1/4
D = max(Dstrength, ceil(Dtwist / 5) × 5)

3.3 Sunk Key Design (IS 2048)

The rectangular sunk key dimensions are proportioned relative to the shaft diameter per IS 2048:

Sunk Key Proportions (IS 2048)
Key Width:   w = ceil(D / 4)   mm
Key Height:   h = ceil(D / 4)   mm
Key Length:   l = 1.5 × D   mm

The key must withstand both shearing and crushing:

Key Stress Verification
Shear failure:
    T = l × w × τk × (D / 2)
    τk = (2 × T) / (D × w × l)   ≤   τallow

Crushing failure:
    T = l × (h / 2) × σck × (D / 2)
    σck = (4 × T) / (D × h × l)   ≤   σc,allow

3.4 Hub & Flange Design (IS 2693 / DIN 116)

All flange and hub dimensions are proportional to the shaft diameter D:

Table 3.1 — Flange & Hub Proportions (IS 2693)
Component Symbol Formula Description
Hub Outer DiameterD12 × DOuter diameter of the cylindrical hub
Hub LengthLhub1.5 × DAxial length of the hub
Pitch Circle DiameterD23 × DBolt arrangement circle diameter
Flange Outside DiameterD34 × DOuter diameter of the flange disc
Flange Thicknesstf0.5 × DAxial thickness of the flange disc
Protective Rim Thicknesstp0.25 × DRaised ring that covers bolt heads

Two shear stress checks are performed on the cast iron flange body:

Flange Body Stress Verification
Hub Torsional Shear (Hollow Cylinder):
    τhub = (16 × T × D1) / [ π × (D1⁴ − D⁴) ]   ≤   15.0 MPa

Flange Circumferential Shear (at hub junction):
    τf = (2 × T) / (π × D1² × tf)   ≤   15.0 MPa

3.5 Fastener (Bolt) Array Design

Bolt Count & Sizing
Number of Bolts:
    i = ceil(D / 50 + 3)

Bolt Core Diameter from Shear Strength:
    T = i × (π/4) × db² × τallow × (D2 / 2)
    db = √[ (8 × T) / (i × π × D2 × τallow) ]

The calculated core diameter is rounded up and matched to the nearest standard ISO metric bolt size (M6, M8, M10, M12, M14, M16, M18, M20, M22, M24, M27, M30, M36, M42).

Empirical Proportion Check:
    db ∈ [ 0.40 × D / √i  ,  0.45 × D / √i ]

Bolt Shear Stress Verification:
    τbolt = (8 × T) / (i × π × D2 × db²)   ≤   τallow

5.2 Step-by-Step Calculations

Step 1: Permissible Stresses

τallow = 380 / (2 × 3.0) = 63.33 MPa
σc,allow = 380 / 3.0 = 126.67 MPa

Step 2: Shaft Diameter (Strength)

d = [(16 × 6,207,000) / (π × 63.33)]1/3 = [99,312,000 / 198.97]1/3 = [499,117.3]1/3
d = 79.3 mm → Standardized D = 80 mm

Step 3: Torsional Rigidity Check

J = (π / 32) × 80⁴ = (π / 32) × 40,960,000 = 4,021,239 mm⁴
θ = (6,207,000 × 1000) / (4,021,239 × 79,000) × (180 / π)
θ = 6,207,000,000 / 317,677,881,000 × 57.296
θ = 0.01954 × 57.296 = 1.120°

Since θ (1.120°) > θmax (1.0°) → Shaft must be upsized for rigidity

Dtwist = [(32 × 6,207,000 × 1000) / (π × 79,000 × 0.01745)]0.25
Dtwist = [198,624,000,000 / 4,328.3]0.25 = [45,889,370.5]0.25
Dtwist = 82.3 mm → Standardized D = 85 mm

Step 4: Key Dimensions (IS 2048)

w = ceil(85 / 4) = 22 mm
h = ceil(85 / 4) = 22 mm
l = 1.5 × 85 = 127.5 mm

τk = (2 × 6,207,000) / (85 × 22 × 127.5) = 12,414,000 / 238,425 = 52.1 MPa ≤ 63.33 ✓
σck = (4 × 6,207,000) / (85 × 22 × 127.5) = 24,828,000 / 238,425 = 104.1 MPa ≤ 126.67 ✓

Step 5: Flange & Hub Dimensions

D1 = 2 × 85 = 170 mm  |  Lhub = 1.5 × 85 = 127.5 mm
D2 = 3 × 85 = 255 mm  |  D3 = 4 × 85 = 340 mm
tf = 0.5 × 85 = 42.5 mm  |  tp = 0.25 × 85 = 21.25 mm

τhub = (16 × 6,207,000 × 170) / [π × (170⁴ − 85⁴)]
      = 16,883,040,000 / [π × (835,210,000 − 52,200,625)]
      = 16,883,040,000 / 2,458,690,547
      = 6.87 MPa ≤ 15.0 ✓

τf = (2 × 6,207,000) / (π × 170² × 42.5) = 12,414,000 / 3,855,761
      = 3.22 MPa ≤ 15.0 ✓

Step 6: Bolt Array

i = ceil(85 / 50 + 3) = ceil(4.7) = 5 Bolts

db = √[(8 × 6,207,000) / (5 × π × 255 × 63.33)]
     = √[49,656,000 / 253,711.3] = √[195.72]
     = 13.99 mm → Rounded to 14 mm → Adopted: M16

Empirical check: [0.40 × 85 / √5, 0.45 × 85 / √5] = [15.2 mm, 17.1 mm] ✓

τbolt = (8 × 6,207,000) / (5 × π × 255 × 14²) = 49,656,000 / 786,072
        = 63.2 MPa ≤ 63.33 ✓

6.2 Web Application (HTML/CSS/JavaScript)

The web application is a single standalone HTML file (~2400 lines) requiring no build tools, frameworks, or external dependencies (except Google Fonts). It features:

  • Three-Column Workspace Layout: Input panel (left), CAD viewport (center), Output dimensions panel (right).
  • Live Reactive Calculations: All sliders, dropdowns, and number inputs trigger instant recalculation and re-rendering.
  • SVG CAD Drawing Engine: Two views — Full Section A-A (longitudinal cross-section) and Flange Elevation (end view) — with ISO hatching patterns, centerlines, dimension arrows, and bolt arrays. A dual drafting sheet mode shows both views.
  • Three Themes: Studio Light (SolidWorks / Fusion 360 style), Classic Blueprint (AutoCAD blue), and Titanium Dark.
  • Torque Calculator: Primary direct torque input with secondary power/speed/overload derivation: T = (P × 60,000) / (2π × N) × Ks.
  • Constraint Telemetry Cards: Live comparison grids showing specified limits vs. mechanically calculated values with PASS/FAIL/UPSIZED badges.
  • Calculation Dossier Modal: Formal engineering report popup with Copy Plain Text and Print/Export PDF buttons.
  • Mouse-Wheel Zoom: Canvas zoom 60%–250% with "Zoom to Fit (100%)" reset button.
